# Phase 5 — human acceptance protocol

**Level 4: NOT RUN. No human enjoyment, difficulty, usability or retention result is claimed.** Automated policies are bounded build heuristics, not human skill labels.

Use Windows Godot 4.7.2 Compatibility, F5 (`scenes/level.tscn`), final Frontier. Record build/configuration hash and shop/spawn/relic seeds. Keep a screen recording if the participant consents. Start with two unfamiliar players and one returning player as a qualitative pilot; this is not a statistically representative sample. Do not teach an optimal layout or require a win.

1. Before play, ask what the participant thinks roads, towers, Gold and the next-wave preview do. Record misunderstandings without correcting them unless they block play.
2. Let them play normally. Preparation is unlimited; measure preparation and combat time separately. Note purchases, rerolls, unspent Gold, skipped cards and reasons. Record any need to wait for or understand a shop animation.
3. After each ACT, ask which threat mattered, whether they could recover from mistakes, and whether the relic choice changed the next purchase or placement. Ask for a brief scarcity rating (too much / comfortable / difficult choice / unable to respond), without supplying a target answer.
4. In ACT II–IV, observe whether they notice taunt/healing/disable and deliberately split roles or load. Ask what the flow preview predicts before the placement, and whether the outcome matches. Record cases where traffic was correctly declined because it could not alter eligible routes or quotas.
5. For Winged Beasts and Scouts, ask the participant to predict the route from the visible road network. Verify that a short path/new route, not threat avoidance, explains it. Note whether towers' directional ranges and mortar misses are legible.
6. Before the boss arrives, ask what the warning/intelligence communicated. During the charge, note whether the player can identify the threatened towers, understand why shooting stopped, and see recovery. Do not describe the boss as balanced because it died.
7. At victory/defeat, check the result, restart once, and verify clean Gold, hand, towers, relics, speed and wave state. Record all input failures, obscured controls or unclear failure causes.

Collect: participant code and prior tower-defense experience; version/seeds; outcome and leak waves; wall-clock run length; decisions that felt meaningful; strongest/weakest tower and reason; unused systems; frustrating or trivial stretches; relic identity; boss readability; three changes they would prioritize. Separate observed behavior, participant quotation and interviewer interpretation. Do not convert a small qualitative pilot into win-rate estimates.

Acceptance gates: a participant can explain why they lost or won; can understand at least one useful split and one ineffective split; can complete transactions and four relic choices without assistance; and sees no restart/cleanup failure. These are usability questions to investigate, not fabricated PASS criteria. If the final encounter dominates otherwise trivial waves, revisit its timing/escort pressure and the preceding ACT pacing before increasing HP globally.
